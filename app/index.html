Why the court rules this way

Perfect universal cloning is impossible

Suppose one unitary U copied every state: U|ψ⟩|0⟩ = |ψ⟩|ψ⟩ and U|φ⟩|0⟩ = |φ⟩|φ⟩. Unitaries preserve inner products, so ⟨ψ|φ⟩ = ⟨ψ|φ⟩². The overlap must therefore be 0 or 1: only orthogonal or identical states can share a perfect copier. This is the content of the no-cloning theorem (Wootters & Zurek, Nature 1982). It is a mathematical result, and this experiment did not produce it.

A CNOT does not clone arbitrary states

CNOT(α|0⟩+β|1⟩)|0⟩ = α|00⟩+β|11⟩. That is an entangled state, not (α|0⟩+β|1⟩)⊗(α|0⟩+β|1⟩). Each output on its own is diag(|α|², |β|²), so for |+⟩ each “copy” has fidelity only 1/2. A CNOT copies |0⟩ and |1⟩ perfectly because they are known and orthogonal. That is classical bit copying, not cloning an unknown quantum state. (Exact calculation; the CNOT is not run in this app.)

The 5/6 bound has precise assumptions

5/6 is the maximal single-copy fidelity for cloning that is deterministic (always succeeds), symmetric (both copies equally good), universal (same quality for every pure qubit input), 1→2, on qubits (Bruß et al., Phys. Rev. A 1998, abstract-level check). Asymmetric, probabilistic, state-dependent or N→M cloning are governed by other results. The 5/6 must not be carried over to them.

A finite experiment does not prove the no-cloning theorem

The theorem speaks about all states and all unitaries. This Run tests one input state, 2000 shots, on an ideal simulator that itself computes quantum mechanics. A run can be consistent with a prediction, or disagree with it statistically. It cannot establish a universal impossibility.

A correct DOI does not prove an unrelated claim

A verified DOI shows that a publication exists with that title, authors and year. It does not show that the paper says what is claimed. Deutsch & Jozsa (1992) is genuine but concerns quantum algorithms. The checked abstract of Bužek & Hillery (1996) does not state the 5/6 optimum. For that, the court requires Bruß et al. (1998).

FLimitations & reproducibility

  • Ideal simulator, not quantum hardware. Nothing here says anything about hardware.
  • One input state in the live experiment (Rz(0.7)·Ry(1.1)|0⟩). Universality comes from the exact calculation, not from the measurement.
  • Scope of the 5/6 optimum: deterministic, symmetric, universal 1→2 qubit cloning only.
  • Finite-shot uncertainty: 2000 shots give intervals about ±0.03 wide. They cannot tell 5/6 from nearby values such as 0.81 or 0.85.
  • False DISPUTED outcomes happen: with 99% family-wise intervals, about 1.4% of runs of this correct circuit still exclude an exact value by chance (measured locally over 2000 seeds).
  • No full-text verification: literature support is title- or abstract-level only.
  • Frozen, manually structured claim register: the claims and their mapping to propositions were written by people, not extracted automatically.
  • Not a general-purpose fact checker: the court rules only on this docket and its building blocks.
  • SHA-256 shows content consistency, not source identity. A matching hash means the text is unchanged. It does not tell you who produced it.
  • Theorem ≠ simulation ≠ observation: the theorem is proved in the literature; the exact values are computed from the circuit; the counts are observed in this Run.
